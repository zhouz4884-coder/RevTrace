of a low-trust return passed current-read-set checks but changed subsequent
answers from 14/16 correct to 0/16 after the model actually consumed the

Each job has an Owner namespace and monotonic issue number; each physical
slot has a worker incarnation, using established RPC identity principles
\cite{birrell1984rpc}. Fencing ends authority to send new evidence while
preserving the original identity of an already accepted complete result.

\paragraph{Local rejection and physical release}
Exact duplicates and fenced messages are rejected locally; they cannot
change selected state or release newer leases. Contradictory currently
owned contents freeze writes. Per-incarnation Pipes and receivers isolate
incomplete frames. Replacement requires process exit, CUDA release and
joined receivers. Complete receipts from the sealed channel are validated
before fencing. A retained result still needs its original full input to
match the selected parent; producer exit supplies no such evidence.

\paragraph{Complete buffers across producer death}
Two matching and two mismatching timing controls isolate a complete
result already registered by the Owner. The fixture holds the parent
after original ASR and the child after sending its complete original
result. We terminate the producer, verify exit, CUDA release and
sealed-channel validation, fence new messages, and release the original
parent correction. Both matching results install and are consumed once
with their original job, birth and incarnation; both mismatching results
are rejected against the actual same-frontier parent. Each positive case
also completes a subsequent independent request. These controlled
interleavings establish result retention and rejection, rather than
fault-time SLOs.
\par\noindent\textbf{Finding 4: producer failure and result invalidity are different.}
Fencing removes future message authority; an acquired result keeps its
original identity and remains usable only under its original obligations.
