# Additional controller action worth testing, not a measured improvement

The input-credit book is an obligation/safety mechanism, not by itself proof
of a better scheduler. Merely switching to CERTIFIED-ONLY at a high age could
lose offered-audio Goodput; a fair queue controller may be equally good.

A minimal action with an observable justification is to retire **not-yet-
dispatched private targets whose original display deadline has already passed**.
They cannot earn first-display deadline credit even if generation were instant.
Keep their audio in accepted/canonical debt and mark every such fine window as
explicitly unserved for private display. Never advance C or relabel an old
hypothesis. Already running GPU work is paid and physically drains normally.

The next legal private job, if any, must be newly frozen using actually released
and accepted audio, eligible current Canonical Parent or a still-legal private
chain, and an actual new target. Its adapter input/chunk advance and generated
quality can differ. Missing intermediate private nodes can make retention
unprovable; in that case show the canonical view. Do not splice old Z or invent
private ancestry. A/B/C/D and trusted selection for canonical writes stay fixed.

This can reduce useless speculative queue work without introducing a new rho
optimizer. But ordinary deadline-expiring queue control must get the same action.
Only extra value from certified debt, actual valid visibility and causally
eligible recovery should be credited to feedback-aware CDE; if a conventional
expiry/backpressure controller matches it, independent algorithm novelty is NO-GO.

Before implementing this live, prove bounded per-window outcome accounting and
that only unissued jobs are skipped; verify concrete adapter/branch legality and
full quality/cost. Evaluate issued-work savings, offered coverage and every old
miss, not just admitted-request latency. This is not a preannounced performance
win or an excuse to remove hard requests from denominators.
