        RevTrace: Dual-Granularity Feedback Transactions for State-Certified Streaming ASR
                                            Serving

     Abstract
     Streaming ASR often feeds generated text into later rounds, so a displayed revision becomes a write to future model state and batch-
     induced differences can persist beyond one response. We formalize this feedback-state contamination channel and confirm it causally:
     among 21 execution-induced output pairs, 8 propagate with feedback and none do when the edge is removed. We introduce Dual-
     Granularity Feedback Transactions (DGFT), which decouple low-latency proposal and display from coarse canonical state-write
     authority. Only a certificate-bearing, versioned transaction can enter future state; same-granularity certification is the 𝜌 = 1 case. We
     prove exclusion of uncertified ancestry, fork-free committed histories, and safety independent of granularity. We evaluate RevTrace
     on Qwen3-ASR 0.6B/1.7B and Whisper tiny/base/small.en on two RTX 3090 GPUs, and an independent eight-V100 contract. On
     a matched Qwen workload, coarse authority improves throughput by 1.39×/1.28× over same-granularity configurations; across
     volatile-mode production contracts RevTrace reaches up to 6.88× wall-clock speedup and passes all 16 Whisper cells. We observe
     no output/state or transaction-install violation over 800 Qwen revision events (480 SG, 320 DGFT) and 1,048 Whisper commits;
     a 250,000-attempt fuzzer accepts all 10,000 valid commits and rejects all 240,000 mutations/replays. On the V100 contract, four
     proposal and four authority replicas sustain median 157.8×/111.7× audio real-time throughput at c64 with display P99 1.089/1.394 s;
     certified-segment commit P99 is separately 3.080/4.890 s, and c128 is retained as the failed display-SLO boundary. State-write
     authority therefore enables safe, high-throughput revision serving; risk schedules verification but cannot authorize installation.
     Keywords: streaming ASR serving, LLM inference, optimistic concurrency control, state certification, transactional runtime,
     feedback state
