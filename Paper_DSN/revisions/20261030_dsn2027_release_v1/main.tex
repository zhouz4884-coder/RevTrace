\title{RevTrace: Preserving Valid Progress across Trusted Feedback Revisions}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous submission}\IEEEauthorblockA{Regular paper}}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Trusted feedback can invalidate speculation while leaving its computation
and delivered progress usable. RevTrace coordinates dependency reuse and
whole-hypothesis consumption around a protected feedback-state contract.
Complete frozen inputs establish whether an executing call remains
eligible; a separate dependency witness governs its whole visible view.
This permits input-confirmed native recognizer--corrector calls whose own
next predictions are wrong, while original correction retains selection
authority. A selected-trace refinement argument connects durable facts and
recovery to the original transition relation. In a predeclared 24-speaker
stratum, computation retention alone increases frontier withdrawals from
12 to 17; joint coordination produces zero and adds 26.645 seconds of
deadline credit over 714.110 seconds offered. Native confirmation on four
unused speakers preserves complete original correction histories and
improves deadline credit in all eight pairs. Paired fail-stop and
sustained-state experiments test local rejection, recovery costs, and
protocol history under native state changes.
\end{abstract}
