\subsection{From physical release to supported service}
\label{subsec:release_evidence}
A 24-run core study crosses $R$ and $K$ with $S$ enabled, two arrival
patterns, and three fresh-process repeats. Its 16 fixed development
sources offer 250.180\,s per run. A frozen wrong-conditioning fixture
creates useless tails, separately from lawful-continuation evidence.
All selected Canonical $F/Z/P/T$ histories match, with WER $14/687$;
all 675 pairs with identical complete inputs, prompt/audio identities,
and peer order return identical outputs.

Under synchronized $R1$, $K$ makes the following 16-row computation
start 473--577\,ms earlier. Request acknowledgment still precedes physical
drain. All 96 following rows across the six synchronized comparisons
are actually consumed and first advance their targets. Their whole
hypotheses match later complete same-frontier Canonical text byte-for-byte,
without worker input overrides. This is selected-model support, not
human transcription accuracy.

Earlier release does not always change a deadline: only 6/6/1 rows under
$R1$ switch from dual-deadline miss to hit. Adding
$K$ to $R1$ changes original credit by +15.255/+13.265/+7.970\,s under
synchronized arrivals and $-26.000/-6.475/+21.780$\,s under staggered
arrivals; model time and board energy do not improve consistently.
Each synchronized arm also has 16\,s of timely first-ahead content
without exact later support, or 14\,s after normalization, leaving these
credit differences intact. Thus $K$ supplies a measured drain-to-service
opportunity, not a stable positive $R\times K$ interaction.
