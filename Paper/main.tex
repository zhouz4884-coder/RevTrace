\begin{abstract}
Streaming ASR often feeds generated text into later rounds, so a displayed
revision becomes a write to future model state and batch-induced differences
can persist beyond one response. We formalize this
feedback-state contamination channel and confirm it causally: among 21 legal
paired interventions, 8 propagate with feedback and none do when the edge is
removed. We introduce Dual-Granularity Feedback Transactions (DGFT), which
decouple low-latency proposal and display from coarse canonical state-write
authority. Only a certificate-bearing, versioned transaction can enter future
state; same-granularity certification is the $\granratio=1$ case. We prove
exclusion of uncertified ancestry, fork-free committed histories, and safety
independent of granularity. RevTrace implements DGFT and is evaluated
on Qwen3-ASR 0.6B/1.7B and Whisper tiny/base/small.en on two RTX~3090 GPUs,
plus an independent eight-V100 scale-out contract. On a matched Qwen workload,
coarse authority improves throughput by $1.39\times$/$1.28\times$ over
same-granularity configurations; across production contracts RevTrace reaches
up to $6.88\times$ wall-clock speedup and passes all 16 Whisper cells. We
observe no output/state or transaction-install violation over 800 Qwen
revision events and 1{,}048 Whisper commits; a 250{,}000-attempt fuzzer accepts
all 10{,}000 valid commits and rejects all 240{,}000 mutations/replays. On the
V100 contract, four proposal and four authority replicas sustain
147.1$\times$/114.5$\times$ audio real-time throughput at c64 with display
P99 1.115/1.471\,s; c128 is retained as the measured 2\,s-SLO boundary. These
results establish state-write authority as a separate contract for safe,
high-throughput revision serving; risk may schedule verification but cannot
authorize installation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Rejected realizations and measurement repairs}\label{app:repairs}
Three deviations were found and resolved during closure, and are documented
with the frozen artifacts.
(i) An FP16 singleton coarse-authority candidate was rejected after failing
its quality contract; FP32 was adopted as the coarse-authority precision
instead (the SG path retains its own FP16 singleton authority under the
$\granratio=1$ protocol, Corollary~\ref{cor:sg}).
(ii) The FP32 KV-cache bound was reduced from 65{,}536 to 4{,}096 tokens
per session.
(iii) A per-row CUDA-synchronization defect in the online submission path
was fixed before the R9.E closure.
All numbers reported in this paper were re-audited after these repairs and
are bound to the release's code commit, contract digest, and result hashes.
