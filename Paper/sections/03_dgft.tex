\subsection{Transactions, certificates, and commits}\label{sec:dgft:tx}

DGFT wraps every state projection in a transaction.

\begin{definition}[DGFT transaction]\label{def:tx}
A transaction is the object
\[
  \tx_t = \bigl(\mathrm{sid},\, e,\, p,\, v_{\mathrm{parent}},\,
  [a_s,a_e),\, \out_t,\, \proj(\out_t;\state_t),\, \mathrm{cert}_t\bigr),
\]
comprising the session id, contract epoch $e$, policy $p$, the parent state
version read, the audio frontier interval covered, the generated output,
its state projection, and a certificate record. The proposal granularity
$g_p$ (fine window) and the authority granularity $g_a$ (coarse canonical
segment) define the granularity ratio $\granratio = g_a/g_p \ge 1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Commit tuple and validity]\label{def:commit}
An authority commit is a tuple
\[
  \commit_j = (\mathrm{sid}_j,\, e_j,\, p_j,\, v_j,\, [f_j,f_{j+1}),\, \cert_j)
\]
with $\cert_j \in \{0,1\}$. $\commit_j$ is \emph{valid}, written
$\mathrm{Valid}(\commit_j)$, iff (i) session, contract epoch, and policy
match the live session fence; (ii) $v_j$ equals the currently committed
parent version; (iii) $[f_j,f_{j+1})$ continues the committed frontier
without gap or overlap; and (iv) $\cert_j = 1$, i.e.\ the projection passed
the contract's authority checks. Only valid commits mutate committed state.
\end{definition}

Definitions~\ref{def:tx}--\ref{def:commit} realize requirements
R1--R4 (Section~\ref{sec:model:reqs}) structurally: display never produces
a commit (R1), $v_j$ enforces parent ownership (R2), $[f_j,f_{j+1})$
enforces frontier monotonicity (R3), and $\cert_j$ is granted only by
authority checks, never by risk signals (R4).

\subsection{Theorem 1: uncertified-ancestry exclusion}\label{sec:dgft:thm1}

\begin{theorem}[Uncertified-Ancestry Exclusion]\label{thm:ancestry}
If future feedback state is updated exclusively through authority commits
satisfying $\mathrm{Valid}$ (Definition~\ref{def:commit}), then no fine
proposal whose output is absent from an authority certificate can become an
ancestor of any future committed state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction over the commit sequence $\commit_1,\commit_2,\ldots$
Base: the initial committed state $\state_0$ is contract-defined, so its
ancestry contains no fine proposal. Step: assume committed state $\state_j$
has ancestry containing only certificate-covered outputs. The next state
$\state_{j+1}$ arises only from a valid $\commit_{j+1}$; validity requires
$\cert_{j+1}=1$, and the certificate enumerates the outputs whose projections
the commit installs. Any fine proposal not covered by that certificate is
therefore absent from the ancestry of $\state_{j+1}$. Provisional display
never mutates committed state by construction of the runtime
(Section~\ref{sec:system}), completing the induction.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ancestry} is deliberately architecture-agnostic: it depends
on the commit protocol, not on margin accuracy, model determinism,
FP16/FP32 agreement, or the choice of ASR family.

\subsection{Theorem 2: committed-state linearizability}\label{sec:dgft:thm2}

\begin{theorem}[Committed-State Linearizability]\label{thm:linear}
Under parent-version checking (Definition~\ref{def:commit}(ii)) and
frontier-continuity checking (Definition~\ref{def:commit}(iii)), the
accepted commits of a session form a fork-free, gap-free, overlap-free
linear history of feedback state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Parent-version checking makes the accepted commits a tree of height at most
one per version: two distinct commits cannot both satisfy
$v_j = \mathrm{version}(\state)$ against the same committed state, so the
history is fork-free. Frontier-continuity checking orders accepted intervals
$[f_j,f_{j+1})$ into a sequence with $f_{j+1} = f'_{j}$ for consecutive
commits, excluding gaps and overlaps. Hence the accepted commits are a
single linear sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stale-descendant rejection]\label{cor:stale}
A revision computed against $v_{\mathrm{parent}} \neq
\mathrm{version}(\state_j)$ cannot legally commit: it fails
Definition~\ref{def:commit}(ii) and is rejected without mutating state.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Theorem 3: granularity-independent safety}\label{sec:dgft:thm3}

\begin{theorem}[Granularity-Independent Transaction Safety]\label{thm:gran}
For any granularity ratio $\granratio \ge 1$, if every authority segment
starts at the committed parent, covers a contiguous audio frontier, yields a
legal state projection, and passes the certificate and version checks of
Definition~\ref{def:commit}, then the conclusions of
Theorems~\ref{thm:ancestry} and~\ref{thm:linear} hold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:ancestry} and~\ref{thm:linear} use only the
validity predicate of Definition~\ref{def:commit}; none of their steps
depends on the size of $[f_j,f_{j+1})$ relative to the fine window. Varying
$\granratio$ changes how much audio a single certificate covers, not which
commits are admissible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Same-granularity certification as $\granratio=1$]\label{cor:sg}
Setting $\granratio = 1$ makes proposal and authority granularities
coincide: the directional coverage witness degenerates to the identity and
fine replay degenerates into risk-triggered re-verification. The
same-granularity (SG) configuration is therefore a reduction of DGFT under
the same protocol, not a separate mechanism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Canonical-path boundary]\label{rem:canonical}
Corollary~\ref{cor:sg} identifies the protocol, not the arithmetic path: in
our contracts SG runs an FP16 singleton authority while Coarse/Hybrid run an
FP32 batched authority. These are distinct canonical paths; we claim
protocol equivalence, never bitwise identity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Authority-cost decomposition}\label{sec:dgft:cost}

The safety results above are silent about cost; the following analysis is a
proposition with explicit assumptions, whose predictions we test
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}, H3). Let $B$ be the number of concurrent sessions,
$q$ the SG verification fraction, $r$ the Hybrid selective-replay fraction,
$C_s$ the singleton authority cost per covered region, $C_c(\granratio,B)$
the batched coarse authority cost per segment, $C_{\mathrm{commit}}$ the
transaction/commit overhead, and $Q_s$ the queueing/fragmentation cost of
singleton authority. Then
\begin{align}
  T_{\mathrm{SG}}^{a} &\approx q\,B\,\granratio\,C_s \;+\; Q_s, \label{eq:cost-sg}\\
  T_{\mathrm{DGFT}}^{a} &\approx C_c(\granratio,B) \;+\;
    r\,B\,\granratio\,C_s \;+\; C_{\mathrm{commit}}, \label{eq:cost-dgft}
\end{align}
and end-to-end service time is governed by the slower lane plus repair:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost-service}
  T_{\mathrm{service}} \approx \max(T_p, T^{a}) + T_{\mathrm{repair}},
\end{equation}
with proposer and authority running concurrently on separate GPUs.

\begin{proposition}[When coarse authority wins]\label{prop:coarse}
Coarse-DGFT improves service throughput over SG when
\[
  C_c(\granratio,B) + rB\granratio C_s + C_{\mathrm{commit}}
  \;<\; qB\granratio C_s + Q_s .
\]
In particular, even a small verification fraction $q$ leaves SG paying
singleton invocation and queueing costs across all $B$ sessions, while a
batched coarse authority amortizes them into $C_c$.
\end{proposition}

Equations~\eqref{eq:cost-sg}--\eqref{eq:cost-dgft} predict, without fitting:
(P1) SG terminal-burst tail failures even at 8--10\% verification;
(P2) coarse authority amortizes invocation/queue costs into higher
throughput; (P3) Hybrid trades replay fraction $r$ for quality at a
performance and energy cost; (P4) the ordering may reverse across models
because $C_c(\granratio,B)$ is model-dependent. Section~\ref{sec:eval}
checks each prediction against the same-workload ladder.

\subsection{Operating-point trade-offs}\label{sec:dgft:tradeoff}

A deployment selects a configuration policy
$\pi \in \{\mathrm{SG},\,\mathrm{Coarse},\,\mathrm{Hybrid}\}$ trading
latency, energy, and quality subject to hard safety:
\[
  \max_{\pi}\; J(\pi) \quad \text{s.t.}\quad
  \mathrm{TxViolation}(\pi) = 0,\;\;
  \text{display/quality caps of the contract},
\]
where $J$ aggregates the contract's latency, energy, and quality objectives.
DGFT supplies the safety-constrained operating envelope; the three evaluated
contracts instantiate three operating points. We do not solve this
optimization online and do not claim any single operating point dominates:
the measured ordering is workload- and model-dependent
(Section~\ref{sec:eval}, H3).
