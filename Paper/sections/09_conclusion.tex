%% Section 9: Conclusion. (outline v2 sec.3.9)

Revision-generative serving turns generated outputs into speculative writes
to future model state. This paper formalized the resulting contamination
channel, proved that separating display authority from state-write authority
via certificate-bearing transactions excludes uncertified ancestry at any
granularity ratio, and showed that the cost of that separation is governed
by an authority decomposition whose predictions we confirmed
experimentally. On two architecture families and five checkpoints, DGFT in
RevTrace reaches up to $6.88\times$ wall-clock speedup with zero
transaction-install violations across 320 Qwen revision events and
1{,}048 Whisper commits, while same-granularity certification appears as the
$\granratio=1$ reduction of the same protocol. Certification is
contract-scoped: risk signals schedule verification but never authorize
installation, and quality evidence never transfers across estimands.
Extending the transaction discipline to other feedback-driven revision
models---including text-LLM and multimodal settings with stateful
generation---is the natural next step.
