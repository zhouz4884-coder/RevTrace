\subsection{H1: state divergence propagates through the feedback edge}
\label{sec:eval:h1}

\emph{Prediction} (Remark~\ref{rem:causal}): under identical future inputs,
batch assignments, and arithmetic realization, setting $\beta_t \equiv 0$
by removing the feedback edge eliminates propagation of the isolated
batch-created state-entry divergence.
\emph{Experiment.} On the Whisper family we enumerated the 21 legal state
pairs and intervened causally: the identical perturbation was applied with
the feedback edge present and with it removed.
\emph{Result.} With feedback, 8 of 21 legal pairs propagate the divergence;
without feedback, 0 of 21. On the 6 persistent pairs the measured tail RAF
(Definition~\ref{def:raf}) reaches 153.
\emph{Verdict.} The prediction is confirmed: the feedback edge is the
propagation channel, and contamination is therefore a state-authority
problem, not a display problem.

\subsection{H2: transaction invariants exclude uncertified ancestry}
\label{sec:eval:h2}

\emph{Prediction} (Theorems~\ref{thm:ancestry}--\ref{thm:gran}): if the
runtime enforces Definitions~\ref{def:cert}--\ref{def:commit}, no install
violation, fork, gap, or overlap occurs in any completed trace.
\emph{Experiment.} Exhaustive online validation of every transaction install
attempt, reported by scope, plus a deterministic protocol-state fuzzer.
(i) DGFT online transaction trace: 320 Qwen revision events (R10
cross-adapter matrix, four DGFT cells) and 1{,}048 Whisper commits (R9.E,
16 cells) and terminal frontier closure.
(ii) SG cells on the identical runtime and workload: the two SG ablation
cells contribute 240 validated events each.
(iii) Protocol fuzzing: 250{,}000 commit attempts over 64 sessions, cycling
through 24 invalid/replay mutation classes (cross-session/epoch/policy,
certificate-field tampering, stale parent, gap/overlap, duplicate and replay)
and a valid class. This CPU test exercises the commit state machine; it is not
a GPU numerical or population failure-rate experiment.
\emph{Result.} In both scopes: zero transaction-install violations, zero
output/state violations and zero stale or replayed results observed; all
sessions closed with monotone terminal frontiers. The evaluated production
traces contained no stale install attempt, so they validate accepted-history
integrity rather than fault-injected stale-rejection coverage. Batch-repeat
and batch-vs-singleton zeros belong to the separate FP32 preflight scope
(Section~\ref{sec:eval:h5}(ii)) and are not pooled
with the online trace evidence.
The fuzzer accepted all 10{,}000 valid commits, rejected all 240{,}000
mutated/replayed attempts, and recorded zero invalid accepts or state-invariant
violations. Its scope excludes Byzantine trusted components, wall-clock
expiry and GPU worker crashes.
\emph{Verdict.} The invariants hold exhaustively on the evaluated traces.
We report this as exhaustive-trace protocol validation; it is not a
failure-probability-zero claim for unobserved workloads.

\subsection{H3: authority-path configuration shapes the performance frontier}
\label{sec:eval:h3}

\emph{Hypotheses} (Proposition~\ref{prop:coarse}, P1--P4): the evaluated
coarse authority path can beat SG on throughput; SG can show terminal-burst
tails even at 8--10\% verification; Hybrid may pay for quality in performance
and energy; and the ordering may reverse across models.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering

  \caption{Same-workload operating points (c16). Coarse-DGFT is the throughput
  point, Hybrid the quality-conservative point, and SG the same-granularity
  certification point; latency scopes are never mixed on one axis.}
  \label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure*}
\emph{Experiment.} Same-workload ladder on the identical 16 bilingual 30\,s
sessions (c16): SG ($\granratio=1$, FP16 singleton authority, verification
fractions 8.3\%/9.6\%), Coarse-DGFT ($\granratio=3$, FP32 batched authority,
$r=0$), Hybrid-DGFT ($\granratio=3$, $r>0$), on Qwen3-ASR 0.6B and 1.7B.
This is a complete-configuration ablation: authority precision and execution
mode change together with granularity. It therefore does not isolate a
precision-independent causal effect of $\granratio$.
\emph{Result} (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}, Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}).
Direct service wall times are 14.905/16.285\,s for SG and
10.759/12.763\,s for Coarse-DGFT, yielding the pairwise throughput ratios
$1.39\times$ (0.6B) and $1.28\times$ (1.7B) and wall-time reductions of
27.8\% and 21.6\%. The separately normalized headline speedups in
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} use each path's frozen all-singleton reference and
must not be divided to recover these pairwise ratios. SG misses its 2\,s
\emph{certified state-entry} deadline at the terminal burst (miss rates
3.33\%/4.17\%; P99 2.735/2.964\,s). Coarse/Hybrid provide provisional
display P99 $\le 1.018$\,s, while their segment-commit P99 is
2.582--4.437\,s. We report these as different latency contracts and do not
transfer a pass/fail label between them. Hybrid holds throughput near SG
($1.03\times$ on 0.6B) at
lower edit rate (2.15\% vs 3.43\% P95) but costs energy
($+16.8\%$ vs SG on 0.6B; $+54.2\%$ on 1.7B, where Hybrid also loses
throughput, $0.85\times$). The direct energy comparison against SG reverses
between models: Coarse saves 4.4\% on 0.6B but consumes 15.1\% more on
1.7B.
\emph{Verdict.} The observations are consistent with P1--P4, including the
model-dependent reversal of the energy ordering, but support a claim about
the measured authority-path configurations rather than granularity alone.
The SG configuration remains the same-granularity certification point
(Corollary~\ref{cor:sg}), not a dominated baseline.

\subsection{H4: one invariant holds across adapters}\label{sec:eval:h4}

\emph{Prediction} (Section~\ref{sec:sys:adapters}): the protocol needs only
projection, authority, and certificate interfaces, so one runtime serves
structurally different revision semantics.
\emph{Experiment and result.} On Qwen3-ASR (rollback, holdback, growing
buffer), the R10 production matrix reaches $6.88\times$ wall-clock and
$3.44\times$ GPU-normalized speedup with 59.8\% energy reduction
(0.6B, Coarse-DGFT), and $6.06\times/3.03\times$ with 51.0\% energy
reduction (1.7B), at P95 mixed-unit edit rates $\le 3.43\%$ against an
independent FP16 singleton reference and display P99 $\le 1.018$\,s. On
Whisper (previous-text conditioning), the identical runtime passes 16/16
production cells with median wall-clock speedup $2.841\times$
(2.633--3.849 across cells), $1.421\times$ GPU-normalized, 31.13\% energy
reduction, display P99 $\le 1.492$\,s, and 1{,}048 certificates with zero
violations.
The 16 cells are explicit rather than pooled: Whisper base.en uses five
synchronous and five Poisson seeds, while tiny.en and small.en each use three
synchronous seeds.
\emph{Verdict.} Confirmed: the invariant transfers across two architecture
families through adapters; performance magnitudes are family- and
model-dependent and are reported per contract.

\subsection{H5: each certification layer guarantees only its scope}
\label{sec:eval:h5}

\emph{Prediction} (R4, Section~\ref{sec:sys:cert}): evidence strata are
non-transferable; each yields a bounded statement about its own scope.
\emph{Result, by stratum.}
(i) SG numerical risk: randomized audits of the SG authority path
($\approx$30k audits, checkpoints 20\,s/120\,s) give anytime-valid UCBs of
0.480\%/0.478\%; this bound belongs to the SG adapter path only and is not
transferred to DGFT paths~\cite{howard2021confidence}.
(ii) Authority implementation: the contract-scoped FP32 preflight records
zero batch-repeat and zero batch-vs-singleton mismatches on the frozen Qwen
contract (10.7--11.1\,s, excluded from service timing).
(iii) Transaction integrity: H2's exhaustive validation (zero observed
violations on 320 DGFT Qwen events, 480 SG Qwen events, and 1{,}048 Whisper
certified commits; no stale attempt occurred in these traces).
(iv) Semantic quality, per family and never pooled: Qwen mixed-unit edit
rate against the independent FP16 singleton reference has P95 $\le 3.43\%$
(worst 5.41\%, zero catastrophic cases; exact sessions rise from 11/16 to
13/16 on 0.6B Hybrid and from 9/16 to 10/16 on 1.7B); Whisper paired WER
deltas against unprotected feedback are all negative, $-12.23$/$-18.21$/$-25.27$
points on dev for tiny/base/small.en (bootstrap uppers
$-10.48$/$-16.17$/$-22.15$) and $-8.26$/$-11.03$/$-13.76$ on the untouched
cohort (40 long sessions per model, deterministically assembled from 214
previously unused source utterances; uppers $-5.15$/$-7.51$/$-9.09$)
(Figure~\ref{fig:quality}).
On the independent 64-utterance-per-model-language closure, deployed Qwen
records English WER 2.15\%/2.30\% (0.6B/1.7B), Chinese CER
2.71\%/2.16\%, terminal exactness 256/256 against singleton authority, zero
catastrophic outliers, and partial-revision edit rates 24.7--31.8\%.

\subsection{H6: faithful revision adaptations expose the missing contract}
\label{sec:eval:h6}

Published systems target different semantic objects, so their headline
numbers are not an apples-to-apples leaderboard. We instead run faithful
\emph{revision-level adaptations} on the same frozen overloaded c32 trace
(Table~\ref{tab:external-adaptations}). A MarginGate-inspired fixed threshold
verifies 3.02\% of events but misses the known state-changing flip; an
owner/version-only baseline rejects stale work but still accepts that legal
current-owner flip. An LLM-42-style adaptation verifies every revision and
eliminates the observed state error at 1.565 events/s. RevTrace's scoped
repair verifies 5.05\%, eliminates the error, and reaches 3.046 events/s
($1.95\times$ the always-verify adaptation). This overloaded trace remains
outside the 2\,s SLO (P99 6.107\,s), so the comparison isolates safety cost
rather than serving capacity. These are adaptations, not end-to-end claims
about the original text-only systems~\cite{chu2026margingate,zhang2026llm42}.

\subsection{H7: proposer--authority scale-out preserves the SLO until the
measured capacity boundary}\label{sec:eval:h7}

\emph{Prediction} (Equation~\eqref{eq:cost-service}): authority-only scaling
cannot remove a proposer bottleneck; balanced proposal/authority replication
should increase service capacity until one stage saturates. Coarse FP32
authority should be most expensive at $\granratio=1$ and become amortizable as
$\granratio$ increases.
\emph{Experiment.} We execute an independent 84-cell matrix on one
8$\times$Tesla-V100-32\,GB node: 24 authority microprofiles, 16 normal-load
c16 baselines, 32 scale cells over c16/c32/c64/c128 and p1a1/p1a2/p2a2/p4a4,
and 12 FP16/FP32 authority-attribution cells at
$\granratio\in\{1,3,5\}$. Each 30\,s c16 baseline contains 240 display events;
each c64 cell contains 960. The complete raw matrix and worker diagnostics are
audited separately from the RTX~3090 contract.

\emph{Result.} First, all repeated batched V1 executions agree, yet each model
accumulates 62 batch--singleton request mismatches across FP16/FP32 and batch
sizes 1--32. FP32 is therefore not an unconditional singleton-equivalence
certificate on this platform. Second, under the normal-load c16 contract,
SG reaches $4.682\times/3.248\times$ wall speedup with 6.67\%/10.00\%
verification and 0\% P95 final edit; Hybrid reaches
$5.764\times/4.657\times$ (Table~\ref{tab:v100-scaling}). Third, p4a4 at c64
sustains $147.138\times/114.488\times$ audio real-time throughput with display
P99 1.115/1.471\,s, zero misses among 960 display events per model, and nominal
scale efficiency 91.1\%/100.7\% relative to p1a1 at the same load. At c128,
throughput saturates at $148.930\times/111.133\times$ while P99 rises to
2.266/2.799\,s; c64 is therefore the measured 2\,s-SLO capacity for both
models. Finally, at $\granratio=1$ the FP32 authority/proposer service ratios
are 2.216/2.669 and throughput falls to $18.603\times/12.294\times$; at
$\granratio=5$ these ratios fall below one (0.759/0.841), restoring
$40.630\times/33.909\times$ at c16. Across the matrix we observe no
transaction-install, uncertified-ancestry, or directional-omission violation.

\emph{Verdict.} The scale and attribution results support the two-stage cost
model: balanced replication, rather than authority replicas alone, expands
capacity, and coarse authority becomes economical only after sufficient
amortization. The result closes the single-node high-concurrency gap but does
not establish multi-node scaling; c128 is reported as a failed SLO boundary.
